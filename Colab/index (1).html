Be an Earth System Trend Detective

Marine Ecosystem Change Explorer

A tool that reads NASA satellite data and tells you what is changing in the ocean, where, how much — and whether it is significant.

01 — The challenge

One variable. Two directions.

Earth's systems change constantly — and the same variable can rise in one region and fall in another, driven by the same process. Averages hide it. Thousands of variables bury it.

What?Where?How much?Significant?

Our test case: the Gulf of Mexico, 276 months (2003–2025) of NASA data — MODIS-Aqua chlorophyll-a and sea surface temperature, GPM IMERG precipitation, and VIIRS chlorophyll to cross-check — across 7 marine regions and 5 river basins.

02 — The tool

From 40 GB of orbits to four answers.

  1. 01 Select — any NASA variable, region and period. Data is clipped on the fly: ~40 GB → 300 MB in minutes.
  2. 02 Clean — monthly anomalies remove the seasonal cycle; chlorophyll in log10.
  3. 03 Measure — Sen's slope per decade with a 95% confidence interval.
  4. 04 Test — Mann-Kendall corrected for autocorrelation (Hamed-Rao), plus ANOVA across periods and regions.
  5. 05 Verify — false-discovery-rate control on ~2,300 map cells, leave-one-year-out, second satellite (VIIRS).

04 — Where it changes

The average hides the story.

Across the whole Gulf, chlorophyll-a — the base of the marine food web — shows no significant trend. Region by region, the story changes: the Campeche Bank and the deep Gulf are losing it.

−7% / decade
Chlorophyll decline on the Campeche Bank (−6.7% MODIS, −7.6% after correction) — about −15% since 2003. Confirmed by MODIS, VIIRS and fluorescence.

Warm months carry less chlorophyll in all 7 regions (r = −0.21 to −0.46).

Rain in river basins reaches coastal chlorophyll 1–3 months later — except in Yucatán, where water flows underground.

05 — Is it significant?

We made the method prove itself.

  • ◆ Monthly data are autocorrelated: 276 months ≈ 54–98 independent values. Correcting it moved Tamaulipas from p = 0.006 to p = 0.09.
  • ◆ A negative control (Mississippi rain → deep Gulf) correctly comes out non-significant.
  • ◆ A second satellite (VIIRS, r = 0.90–0.96) exposed a MODIS sensor drift: the “greener coasts” vanished, and a decline in the deep Gulf appeared.
  • ◆ Every result is graded solid · weak · not detected: 10 of 19 trends are significant.
06 — Why it matters

An early warning for a warming sea.

Fisheries · Campeche Bank
Home of the Mayan octopus and grouper — warms among the fastest (+0.53 °C/decade) and loses the most chlorophyll (−7%/decade).
Coral reefs
+1 °C raises bleaching risk at Alacranes Reef and the Veracruz Reef System.
Hurricanes
A warmer Gulf can fuel rapid intensification near Tamaulipas, Veracruz, Tabasco and Yucatán.
Rivers · Grijalva-Usumacinta
Basin rain fell 102 mm/yr per decade; it reaches coastal chlorophyll one month later (r = 0.48).

Chlorophyll trends need 30–60 years of continuous data to detect — continuity from MODIS to VIIRS and PACE is essential.
